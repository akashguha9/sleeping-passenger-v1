**Major risks:** the RBI held the repo rate at 5.25% in June but turned cautious — cutting FY27 GDP growth guidance to 6.6% (from 6.9%) and raising the inflation forecast to 5.1% (from 4.6%) on LPG/metals/plastics costs, while announcing rupee-support measures explicitly to defend the currency against oil-driven pressure; FII/DII flows are choppy and directionally weak, suggesting a domestically-liquidity-driven, news-reactive tape rather than a one-way conviction inflow; the live SEBI forensic-fraud investigation into Rajesh Exports (alleging ~99.8% of five years' consolidated revenue could not be independently verified) is the standout governance red flag this cycle.

**Opportunity environment:** genuinely constructive but narrow and news-reactive — the strongest ideas this cycle are hard-data-backed (real order wins, real earnings beats, a real IPO-driven re-rating of exchange infrastructure) rather than macro-beta plays, given the market is still digesting a geopolitical oil shock mid-week.

**Weak / risk sectors:** Constellation Energy, despite its 20-year, 1,121 MW nuclear PPA with Meta, is down roughly 35% in H1 2026 — proof that even structurally favored "AI-power toll bridge" suppliers are not immune to sharp valuation resets. Fiserv saw real leadership churn and reports of a possible ~$15B debit-network sale, a reminder that even payment-rail incumbents can see their toll position renegotiated (researched but excluded from the primary list given unresolved structural uncertainty about what the company even remains post-divestiture).

**Major catalysts:** big-bank Q2 earnings kick off July 14; Fed policy under new chair Kevin Warsh held the funds rate at 3.50-3.75% in June with a hawkish dot-plot shift (2026 year-end median near 3.8%); May CPI ran 4.2% YoY, a three-year high; prediction markets put odds of a July 28-29 FOMC hold above 85%, with Goldman now pushing first cuts out to 2027.

**Major risks:** valuation concentration in AI-infrastructure names leaves little room for a capex-growth-rate scare; China export-control exposure (Applied Materials' new BIS Affiliates Rule hit) is a live, name-specific risk even within an otherwise-favored sector; SK Hynix's new Nasdaq listing creates a fresh, liquid comparison point that could pressure Micron's relative valuation.

**Opportunity environment:** constructive and broadening beyond mega-cap GPU names into the power/networking/EDA "picks-and-shovels" layer, but increasingly crowded and momentum-heavy in the most AI-adjacent names — the strongest genuinely differentiated ideas this cycle came from a deliberate second research pass into mid/small-cap, non-mega-cap names (defense electronics, water infrastructure, midstream energy, agricultural finance, medtech) that are not already priced for AI-capex perfection.

**"Momentum-flow buying as fundamental validation" (United States):** Kratos Defense's re-rating includes Ark Invest buying roughly $9.1M in July — genuine, but momentum/thematic-ETF buying is itself often a lagging, sentiment-following signal rather than independent fundamental proof, and should not be read as confirmation on top of the (real) contract-and-guidance evidence already in hand.

**Gambler's-fallacy check on the memory supercycle (both markets):** "the last memory upcycle produced huge winners, so this one will too" is precisely the reasoning the brief flags as a gambler's fallacy. SK Hynix's own $26.5bn capital raise funds new supply; Korea has separately committed roughly $520bn to new fab/HBM capacity system-wide. The mechanism (AI-driven HBM demand) is real today, but the historical pattern in memory has always been supply eventually catching demand — the "structural" and "cyclical" cases have not yet been distinguished by any name in this pool.
