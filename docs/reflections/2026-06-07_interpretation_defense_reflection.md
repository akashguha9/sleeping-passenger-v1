## 0. Executive Thesis

Humans do not act on raw reality. They act on interpreted signals shaped by
context, fear, ROI, familiarity, algorithmic exposure, embodied feeling, and
distribution.

    Action = f(Signal, Context, Feeling, Interpretation, Distribution, Trust)

Strategic lesson for the MVP: **Do not merely collect data. Detect whether the
interpretation layer is clean, context-aware, stress-tested, and economically
meaningful.**

The strongest extracted framework:

    Decision Quality = f(Data Quality, Interpretation Quality, Context Fit,
                         Stress Robustness, Incentive Awareness)

## 1. Core Insights (condensed)

1. Meaning is context-dependent: `M = f(S, C, A)` (signal, context, audience).
2. Surface signal ≠ true meaning: `Signal Quality = Surface × Context Validity × Stress Robustness`.
3. Trashy interpreted data is more dangerous than no data: `Decision Risk = Data Error + Interpretation Error + Context Error`.
4. Fake news spreads through ambiguity, not only falsehood: `Misinfo Risk = P(Misread) × Virality × Emotional Intensity`.
5. Markets sell interpreted feeling, not objective truth: `Behavior = f(Feeling created by data)`.
6. Fear + ROI are basic marketing engines: `EV = P(Success) × Payoff − Cost`; works when `EV_perceived > Cost`.
7. Taleb's luck surface area applies to coaching/networking/products: `LSA = N × Q × R`.
8. Data-driven ≠ context-correct: engagement needs category familiarity.
9. Algorithmic exposure creates category blindness: `Awareness_{i,c} = Σ Exposure × Attention`.
10. Lived context determines interpretation accuracy.
11. Metrics deceive outside their native environment: `True Performance = Metric × Environment Fit`.
12. Stress reveals hidden variables: `Q_true = f(Q_normal, S)`; `S > T_material ⇒ Failure`.
13. Surface similarity ≠ functional equivalence.
14. Meme coins are extreme interpreted-data failure (hype as value).
15. Not every scam is a technical honeypot; some are economic honeypots.
16. Distribution can matter more than product quality: `Outcome = Quality × Distribution × Timing`.
17. Ecosystem/portfolio/distribution is the basic engine: `R = Σ A_i × M_i × C_i`.
18. Product value often comes from embodied feeling.
19. Risk level determines attention level: `Attention = f(Perceived Downside)`.
20. Familiarity beats abstract superiority.

## 10. One-Line Truths (selected)

- People act on interpreted data, not raw data.
- A signal can be real and still mean the wrong thing.
- Context is the meaning engine, not decoration.
- No lived context, no clean interpretation.
- A metric is only valid inside the environment that produced it.
- Stress reveals the hidden variable.
- Distribution can make weak substance look powerful.
- The best model asks who benefits if you believe the signal.
- The MVP's edge is interpretation defense, not data collection.

## 11. Structural Summary

People, markets, and algorithms respond to interpreted signals shaped by
context, feeling, exposure, fear, ROI, and distribution — not raw facts. The MVP
should not merely collect financial data; it should detect context failure,
narrative-substance gaps, distribution-led illusions, audience misinterpretation,
and stress fragility before converting signals into buy/no-buy advisory output.

## 12. Distilled MVP directive

For every signal, ask: what does it mean literally? what does the market think
it means? what else could it mean? who profits if I believe it? what context is
missing? what happens under stress? is the feeling backed by reality? — all
advisory-only; the human execution gate stays LOCKED.
