\# Narrative Intent Cross-Domain Examples



This file shows how the same framework behaves across domains without pretending the domains are identical.



\---



\## Example 1 — Social Media / Meme



\### Artifact

Dating-app style image with a high-status Paris/Eiffel Tower visual and a “truth and a lie” caption.



\### Hook

\- strong visual status

\- aesthetic credibility

\- familiar romance / aspiration frame



\### Distortion

\- low-status, absurd, destabilizing caption inside a credible frame

\- strong frame-content mismatch



\### Trap

\- unresolved binary

\- forced participation

\- identity ambiguity



\### Likely target behavior

\- engage

\- judge

\- re-read

\- share / discuss



\### Likely beneficiaries

\- poster

\- engagement-seeking persona

\- algorithmic distribution



\### Narrative class

\- engineered\_narrative

\- recruitment / engagement adjacent



\### Notes

This is the clearest Hook -> Distort -> Trap example.

\## Example 2 — Markets



\### Artifact

“AI stocks will dominate everything” appearing after a strong price run.



\### Hook

\- legitimate sector growth

\- strong consensus narrative

\- familiar innovation frame



\### Distortion

\- contradiction between optimism and valuation / cycle risks

\- omission of fragility



\### Trap

\- participants forced into belief tension:

&#x20; - momentum is real

&#x20; - contradiction is also real



\### Likely target behavior

\- buy

\- chase

\- hold late

\- defer skepticism



\### Likely beneficiaries

\- existing holders

\- distributors

\- narrative amplifiers

\- sometimes diffuse institutional winners



\### Narrative class

\- could be engineered\_narrative

\- could be distribution

\- could also be emergent if distributed socially rather than centrally designed



\### Notes

Timing matters heavily here. Domain adapter is essential.

\## Example 3 — Football Messaging



\### Artifact

Club statement: “The manager has our full backing.”



\### Hook

\- official club voice

\- institutional legitimacy

\- calming formal tone



\### Distortion

\- may conceal fragility

\- may stabilize panic while true state is uncertain



\### Trap

\- fans and media are pushed into inference:

&#x20; - genuine support?

&#x20; - temporary cover?

&#x20; - buy-time message?



\### Likely target behavior

\- calm down

\- reduce panic

\- align message

\- wait



\### Likely beneficiaries

\- club leadership

\- manager

\- dressing room stability

\- media control interests



\### Narrative class

\- stabilizing

\- possibly confusion\_masking

\- possibly protective



\### Notes

Not all distortion here is exploitative. Could be system-protective.

\## Example 4 — Interpersonal



\### Artifact

Person says: “I’m fine.”



\### Hook

\- ordinary trusted social frame

\- expected conversational shorthand



\### Distortion

\- surface calm may conceal inner complexity

\- simplification reduces friction



\### Trap

\- listener may sense mismatch but lacks closure

\- ambiguity is socially constrained



\### Likely target behavior

\- stop probing

\- maintain surface harmony

\- defer conflict



\### Likely beneficiaries

\- speaker self-image

\- immediate social stability

\- interaction smoothness



\### Narrative class

\- protective

\- stabilizing

\- sometimes emergent self-regulation



\### Notes

This is a small-scale narrative intent case, not a propaganda case.



\---



\## Cross-Domain Warning



The same framework can be applied across domains, but the domains are not identical.



\### What transfers

\- hook

\- distortion

\- trap / ambiguity

\- timing

\- beneficiary mapping

\- target behavior inference



\### What does not transfer automatically

\- execution constraints

\- institutional incentives

\- trust structures

\- pricing / capital flows

\- social norms

\- time horizons



The framework is portable.

The domain mechanics are not automatically portable.
