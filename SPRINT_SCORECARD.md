## What I did NOT do (and why)

- **No threshold tuning against the 37-trade sample.** Every parameter (cost-table values, regime gate, country bar) is justified by principle, not by "raises the backtest number."
- **No safety floor relaxed.** The 8.6 LPQ floor is untouched. The preflight gate became *stricter* (fail-closed on subcheck unavailable), never looser.
- **No tests skipped or `xfail`'d as a shortcut.** All previously-failing tests pass through fixed root causes. Where existing tests assumed behavior I had to change, I rewrote the test to pin the right invariant (and explained why in the commit).
- **No ETFs padded into the country count.** Saudi Arabia / UAE got documented gaps, not fake "single-name" picks.

## What still blocks real money

The infrastructure is now honest, but the **economic case is not yet made**. The forward shadow log holds 0 resolved trades and 0 regimes — `readiness().ready == False` and will be until the operator runs the system live (advisory-only) long enough to accumulate ≥100 trades across ≥2 distinct regimes. Until that bar clears, every in-sample metric this repo can emit is directional only, and the cost model — even at conservative defaults — strongly suggests that at €50 notional the booking plan's 3–4 commissioned fills eat the cited €0.62 gross edge. Real money requires either (a) larger position sizes that amortize commission minimums, or (b) a per-broker cost calibration that materially lowers the floor, or (c) a strategy variant that fires fewer commissioned legs. None of those decisions can be made from this sprint's data; they can be made from the forward log's first 100 resolved trades.
